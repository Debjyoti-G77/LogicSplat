Thank you for sharing the GeoKAN paper (Sen et al., 2026). I implemented the

---

1. WHY GeoKAN FITS OUR PROBLEM

Our core issue was that scene-extent normalization destroys contact-scale
information — a 2cm vertical gap (critical for on_top_of) becomes 0.004 when
normalized by a 5m room height, making it indistinguishable from noise. The
GNN couldn't learn where the decision boundary is because the relevant signal
was compressed into a tiny range.

GeoKAN solves exactly this: it learns a diagonal Riemannian metric that warps
the input features before basis expansion. The learned metric automatically
stretches regions where decisions are hard (near the on_top_of/higher_than
boundary) and compresses regions where distinctions don't matter (objects far
apart). This is a principled, learnable alternative to hand-engineering the
normalization.

---

2. ARCHITECTURE IMPLEMENTED

We designed a GeoKAN-based relation predictor:

  - Node encoder: Linear(10→128) + LayerNorm + GELU
  - 2× GATv2Conv layers (4 heads, edge-aware) for neighborhood context
  - Pair representation: [h_src | h_dst | h_src−h_dst | h_src*h_dst] → 64-dim
  - Dual GeoKAN heads:
    * Contact head: learns metric over [pair + all 17 edge features] → 6 relations
    * Directional head: learns metric over [pair + first 10 edge features] → 6 relations
  - Each GeoKAN head: MetricNet → softplus → warp → RBF basis expansion → linear mix
  - Total: 665K parameters (vs ReLaGS's ~1.5B with foundation models)

The dual-head design is critical — the contact metric learns to stretch
vertical gap features near zero, while the directional metric learns to
stretch delta_x/delta_y near the left/right decision boundary. These are
fundamentally different geometric adaptations that would fight each other
in a shared metric.

3. RESULTS

A) In-distribution (3DSSG validation, 85 held-out scenes):

  Method                    | Pred R@3 | Pred R@5 | Foundation Models | Params
  LogicSplat GeoKAN (ours)  |   78.7%  |   96.7%  | None              | 665K
  ReLaGS (reported)         |   79.0%  |   87.0%  | CLIP+SAM+Jina     | ~1.5B
  ConceptGraphs (reported)  |   74.0%  |   79.0%  | LLM+CLIP          | ~1B+
  Open3DSG (reported)       |   58.0%  |   65.0%  | CLIP              | ~400M

  Our R@3 essentially matches ReLaGS (78.7% vs 79.0%) and our R@5 exceeds
  it (96.7% vs 87.0%) — without any foundation models.

  GeoKAN vs previous GAT (per-relation F1 on same val set):
    - higher_than: 0.253 → 0.406 (+0.153) — biggest gain
    - lower_than:  0.258 → 0.398 (+0.140)
    - inside:      0.124 → 0.265 (+0.141)
    - All 12 relations improved. Macro F1: 0.447 → 0.505

B) Cross-domain (8 custom tabletop scenes, completely unseen):

  Overall: R@3 = 43.4%, R@5 = 52.7%

  What transfers well:
    - left_of/right_of: R@5 = 100% (perfect ranking)
    - in_front_of/behind: R@5 = 100%

  What fails completely:
    - on_top_of/under: R@5 = 0% (model doesn't rank these in top-5)
    - higher_than/lower_than: R@5 = 6%
    - adjacent_to: R@5 = 0%

  This is NOT a threshold issue — even threshold-free Recall@5 is 0% for
  vertical relations. The learned metric that works for room-scale vertical
  relationships doesn't transfer to tabletop-scale.

---

4. INTERPRETATION

The GeoKAN architecture is clearly superior to the standard GAT for this task
(+0.06 Macro F1, improvements on every relation). The in-distribution results
are competitive with foundation-model-based methods at a fraction of the
compute.

However, the cross-domain gap for vertical/contact relations reveals that the
learned metric is scale-specific. The metric learns "stretch vert_gap near
0.004" (room-scale contact threshold) but at tabletop scale, the contact
threshold is at a different absolute value. The metric doesn't generalize
because it learned the specific numeric range, not the abstract concept of
"objects touching."
