Dear Sir,

I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to give you an honest update on the LogicSplat project, the challenges I am facing, and to seek your guidance on the research direction going forward. I have also attached a detailed progress report for your reference.

---

WHAT HAS BEEN DONE
---

The core pipeline is functional end-to-end:
- Smartphone video → NerfStudio → Gaussian Splat → HDBSCAN clustering → Object detection → Relation prediction → Scene graph output

I have trained a dual-head Graph Attention Network (GAT) on ScanNet data with:
- 17-dimensional edge features (including contact-specific and rule-margin features)
- Asymmetric focal loss for rare contact relations
- Per-relation threshold tuning
- Hierarchical constraint enforcement
- Honest train/test splits (fixed a data leakage bug from earlier)

The model achieves Macro F1 = 0.894 on ScanNet test data, with all 12 relations above 0.80 F1. This is a strong result on the training distribution.

I also built a proper evaluation protocol following 3D scene graph literature standards (3DSSG, SceneGraphFusion) that separates clustering quality from relation prediction quality.

---

THE PROBLEMS
---

1. THE DOMAIN GAP (Primary Issue)

The GNN trained on ScanNet (room-scale, 3-8m scenes) completely fails on real tabletop scenes (0.3-0.8m):
- ScanNet test: Macro F1 = 0.894
- Real tabletop scenes: Micro F1 = 0.281

I attempted fine-tuning on 200 synthetic tabletop scene graphs (generated with physics-aware placement and noise injection). This improved the GNN to 0.322, but it still does not beat simple hand-crafted geometry rules (0.478).

The geometry rules — which are just if-statements checking centroid positions and bounding box overlaps — outperform the trained neural network on real scenes. This is the core problem I cannot solve.

2. CLUSTERING IS BROKEN WITHOUT GUIDANCE

HDBSCAN consistently over-segments real scenes (finds 7-8 clusters instead of 4-5 objects). When I give it the correct object count as a hint, it works well (96.9% object recall). Without the hint, end-to-end performance drops from 0.478 to 0.163 — a 3x collapse entirely due to clustering errors.

I cannot use foundation models (SAM, Gaussian Grouping) for clustering because the project's identity is "foundation-model-free." But without them, clustering is unreliable.

3. CIRCULAR LABELS

ScanNet does not have spatial relation annotations. I derive them from geometry rules applied to ground truth bounding boxes. This means the GNN is trained to approximate the geometry rules — it can never exceed them on the same distribution. The GNN is learning to mimic rules, not learning spatial understanding.

4. 70% OF THE WORK ALREADY EXISTS

After reviewing the literature, I found:
- ReLaGS (CVPR 2026) does exactly "Gaussian splat → scene graph" but with CLIP/foundation models
- GaussianGraph (2025) does the same with 2D foundation models + correction modules
- 3DSSG, SceneGraphFusion already established GNN-based relation prediction from 3D data

What remains unique about LogicSplat is the "no foundation models" constraint. But this constraint is also what makes the results worse. I am struggling to find a genuine novelty that is both achievable and publishable.

5. EVALUATION IS THIN

I only have 8 manually annotated test scenes. Bootstrap confidence intervals are wide. I have 19 additional OCTScenes processed but cannot annotate them because I cannot reliably identify which cluster corresponds to which physical object without a visualisation tool.

WHAT I NEED GUIDANCE ON
---

1. RESEARCH DIRECTION

I am at a crossroads. The "foundation-model-free" constraint makes the system worse than existing methods. Should I:
   a) Keep the constraint and frame the paper as "characterising what's achievable without foundation models" (honest but potentially weak for publication)?
   b) Relax the constraint partially (e.g., use SAM only for clustering, keep relation prediction foundation-model-free)?
   c) Change the research question entirely — perhaps focus on something that existing papers do NOT do well?

2. NOVELTY

What could make this publishable given that ReLaGS and GaussianGraph exist? Some directions I am considering:
   a) Focus on the domain gap finding itself — "why room-scale models fail on tabletop and what it takes to fix it"
   b) Focus on Gaussian physical features (covariance eigenvalues → planarity, support likelihood) as a novel signal source
   c) Focus on symbolic consistency repair (deterministic constraint propagation) as a lightweight alternative to learned reasoning
   d) Focus on the evaluation methodology (separating clustering from relation prediction, which existing papers don't do cleanly)
   e) Something else entirely that I am not seeing

3. ARCHITECTURE CHANGE

I am willing to change the architecture completely if it leads to better results. The current approach (HDBSCAN → bbox features → GNN) may be fundamentally limited. Should I consider:
   a) Operating directly on Gaussian-level features (not cluster-level) for relation prediction?
   b) Using a different clustering approach (spectral clustering, learned clustering)?
   c) A completely different pipeline architecture?

4. FINE-TUNING DATA

You mentioned fine-tuning on 15-20 general tabletop scenes. I tried synthetic feature generation (200 scenes) but the features are too clean — they lack real Gaussian noise. The full BlenderProc pipeline (render → NerfStudio → real splats) would give realistic training data but requires significant setup. Is this worth pursuing, or should I focus elsewhere?

5. PUBLICATION TARGET

Given the current state, what venue and format should I target? A full conference paper seems out of reach given the results. Would a workshop paper (IROS, ICRA, 3DV workshop) be appropriate? Or should I aim for something else?

The system works as a prototype. The pipeline is complete, the code is clean, the evaluation is rigorous. But the results do not yet tell a compelling story:

I believe there is a publishable contribution somewhere in this work — whether it is the domain gap characterisation, the physical feature analysis, the evaluation methodology, or something I have not yet identified. But I need your guidance on where to focus my energy to get there.
